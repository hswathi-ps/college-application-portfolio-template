# Resources, Tools & Programs

*Note: verify current URLs and program details each year — offerings,
deadlines, and eligibility criteria change annually. Most of the programs
below are national/multi-state with a local or state-level chapter — search
"[your state/city] + [program name]" to find the actual local contact. The
"Programs Matched to Career Interest" section below is generated from the
"Career interest" field in the profile ("Edit Profile" on the dashboard) — a
curated starting point to search from, not an exhaustive or current list.*

## Standardized Testing
- Khan Academy Official SAT Practice (free, personalized via College Board)
- College Board Bluebook app (official digital SAT delivery + free practice tests)
- ACT official practice tests (act.org)
- Local public library — often has free SAT/ACT prep book collections

## Internships & Programs — Timing at a Glance

Most paid internships require rising juniors/seniors; volunteer programs and
pre-college institutes are often open earlier. Adapt this table to what you
find locally (also reflected as checklist items in `06-Timeline/master-timeline.md`).

| Program type | Earliest grade | When to apply |
|---|---|---|
| Local volunteer program tied to spike (e.g. teen court, hospital volunteering) | 9th, often ongoing | Anytime — contact the coordinator |
| State mock trial / academic competition team | 9th-10th | Fall, when the school year's team forms |
| Local university pre-college institutes | 9th-12th | Often open Nov 1 for the next summer |
| State-run selective summer program (e.g. a Governor's School) | Usually current 11th grade only | Nominated by school, fall of 11th grade |
| Paid local internship program | Rising 11th/12th | Fall of 11th grade, through the school |
| Boys State / Girls State (American Legion) | Rising senior | Nominated in winter/spring of 11th grade |
| City/county government internships | Rising 11th/12th | Rolling, check the city/county site each spring |

## Civic, Academic & Field-Specific Programs

These are genuinely national programs with state or local chapters — search
for your state's version of each:

- **State Mock Trial Program** — most states run a high school mock trial
  competition through the state bar association or a nonprofit partner. Ask
  the school counselor whether a team exists; if not, this is a legitimate
  founding-a-club opportunity (see `03-Extracurriculars/activity-strategy.md`).
- **Youth and Government (YMCA)** — a civics program active in most states;
  students write and debate bills in a model legislature at a state
  conference. Confirm which local Y chapter runs it.
- **We The People: The Citizen and the Constitution** — national
  civics/constitutional-law competition; pairs well with AP World/US History.
- **National History Day** — research-project competition; also pairs well
  with AP World/US History.
- **Boys State / Girls State** (American Legion / American Legion Auxiliary)
  — week-long, hands-on model state government program for rising seniors,
  run in every state. A strong leadership credential regardless of field.
- **DECA** — business/entrepreneurship competitions, national organization
  with school chapters; useful if the student's spike has a business or
  marketing angle.
- **Field-specific olympiads/competitions** — most academic fields have a
  national competition (Science Olympiad, Model UN, robotics leagues, debate
  leagues, National History Day, etc.) — search for the one matching the
  student's spike.
- **Local university pre-college summer institutes** — many universities near
  you likely run week-long, not-for-credit summer programs for high schoolers
  in specific fields (law, business, STEM, the arts). These are usually far
  less selective than national programs and a good way to get a real taste of
  a field. Application windows often open around November 1 for the following
  summer.
- **A state-run selective summer program** (e.g. a state Governor's School) —
  many states run a free, competitive summer enrichment program, nominated by
  the school, usually only open to current juniors. Ask the counselor.

## Local Internships & Business/Entrepreneurship Support

- **Your city or county's paid teen internship program** — many mid-size
  cities run one (through a workforce development office or a local nonprofit
  partner), typically for rising juniors/seniors, sometimes with a stipend.
  Search "[Location] teen internship program."
- **Your city or county government's own internship listings** — many
  municipal governments post paid or unpaid internships open to high schoolers
  with a minimum GPA.
- **A local startup accelerator or innovation hub** — if your area has one,
  check whether it runs any youth/teen entrepreneurship programming.
- **SCORE or SBA small-business resources** — free mentors and basic
  small-business guidance (licensing, IP) if a creative/entrepreneurial
  activity becomes an actual micro-business.

## College Research
- Each school's official Common Data Set (search "[school name] common data
  set") — the most reliable source for actual admit rates, test score ranges,
  and GPA distributions, more accurate than third-party ranking sites
- Net Price Calculators — every school is required to host one; use them once
  the list firms up rather than guessing from sticker price
- Target schools' admissions office virtual info sessions (usually free,
  register on the official admissions site)

## People to Loop In
- **School counselor** — central hub for transcripts, recommendation letters,
  and school-specific opportunities; build this relationship starting now,
  not senior year. Also usually the required contact for internship programs,
  state civic-leadership nominations, and any school-nominated summer program.
- **Club/activity advisors** — ask directly about leadership pathways and
  competition opportunities
- **Local professionals in the student's field of interest** (family or
  community network) — even one shadowing day is valuable, real material
