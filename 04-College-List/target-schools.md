# College List

## Inspiration Schools

List the schools that first got the student excited about college, and note
*why* — specifically. Vague prestige isn't useful here; what department,
location, curriculum, or resource actually matches the spike from
`03-Extracurriculars/activity-strategy.md`?

- **[School 1]** — [what specifically fits: curriculum, location, department, resources]
- **[School 2]** — [what specifically fits]

Note: very few majors are actually required for a given career path (there's
no undergraduate "pre-law" major anywhere, for instance, and most competitive
graduate/professional paths care about a rigorous major and a strong GPA more
than the major's exact title). Don't let an assumed-required major narrow the
list prematurely — research this per field before ruling schools in or out.

## Building a Balanced List

A responsible list has real reach, target, and likely categories — even with
excellent stats, the most selective schools admit in the single digits, so the
list needs schools the student would be genuinely excited to attend at every
tier. Treat any list built before junior year as a first draft to refine as
the student's profile, testing, and preferences develop.

## What to Actually Do With This List (9th-10th grade)

- Don't finalize anything yet — this is a placeholder shape, not a decision.
- Visit any low-cost/local campuses over the next year or two — seeing any
  campus, even one the student won't apply to, helps them learn what they
  like (urban vs. college-town, size, vibe).
- If a family trip goes anywhere near a reach school, tour it — even an
  unofficial walk-through helps test whether an "inspiration" school matches
  the reality.
- Revisit and rebuild this list seriously starting **spring of 10th grade**,
  once PSAT 10 data and two years of grades exist.

## What Each Inspiration School Actually Looks For

- **Rigor**: the most demanding course load the student's school offers,
  sustained across 4 years.
- **A clear voice in essays**: selective schools reward specific, personal
  answers over generic statements. The material from the student's actual
  spike (see `03-Extracurriculars/` and `05-Essays-and-Portfolio/`) is exactly
  the kind of specific, unusual detail that makes an essay memorable.
- **Demonstrated interest in the intellectual area, not just the
  "prestige"**: real activity matters more than the interest area appearing
  as a word in an essay.
- **Full-context evaluation**: most selective schools use holistic review —
  grades, rigor, essays, recommendations, activities, and (where required)
  test scores are all read together, not scored on a formula.

## Financial Planning Note

Many highly selective schools meet 100% of demonstrated financial need, and a
number have gone tuition-free or near-free for families under certain income
thresholds. Check each school's current net price calculator once the list
firms up — these policies are genuinely generous and shouldn't be assumed out
of reach based on sticker price alone.
