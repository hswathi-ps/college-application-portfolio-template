# Extracurricular Strategy

## The Principle: Depth Over Breadth

Admissions readers see thousands of applicants with long activity lists. What
stands out is a clear, sustained "spike" — one or two threads pursued with
real depth, leadership, and (ideally) some outside-of-school validation or
impact — rather than ten clubs with no follow-through. The job over the next
four years is to identify the one or two threads that matter most to the
student and deepen those, letting everything else be enjoyed without needing
to "count."

## Step 1: Find the Spike

Answer these honestly before filling in anything else in this file:

- What does the student already do without being asked — the thing they'd
  keep doing even if no college ever saw it?
- Where does an academic/career interest (e.g. law, medicine, engineering,
  business, the arts) intersect with something they're genuinely good at or
  drawn to outside the classroom?
- Is there a "real-world" version of this interest nearby — a competition, a
  volunteer program, a local professional willing to have them shadow, a
  mentor?

For many students, the strongest spike sits at the intersection of an
academic/career interest and a creative or entrepreneurial pursuit — not
because that combination is inherently better, but because it's specific and
hard to fake. Fill in the real answer for your student, not a generic one.

## Building the Spike: Two Recommended Threads

Once you've identified the spike from Step 1, sketch two threads here — one
tied to the primary interest area, one tied to a creative/entrepreneurial or
complementary pursuit if one exists.

### Thread 1 — [Primary Interest, e.g. Law & Civic Engagement / STEM Research / Healthcare]
- Identify the school club or competition that maps most directly onto this
  interest, and aim for a leadership role in it by 10th-11th grade.
- Look for a "real-world" version: shadowing, volunteering, an internship, a
  local competition — even a few hours of direct exposure gives real material
  to write about, versus abstract interest in the subject.
- Look for a national competition or program that pairs with this interest
  (subject-specific olympiads, research competitions, civic programs — see
  `07-Resources/links-and-tools.md`).

### Thread 2 — [Creative/Entrepreneurial Thread, if one exists]
- Treat it as a real, ongoing body of work: document it (see
  `05-Essays-and-Portfolio/`).
- Look for a competition, club, or venture structure (business plan
  competitions, a small shop, a portfolio, a publication) that gives it a
  concrete, resume-worthy result.
- If there's a school course (CTE, art, business, etc.) that adds transcript
  weight to match the activity, take it.

### Threads to Keep Without Forcing Into the "Spike"
- Team sports, ongoing music/dance/art training, and other long-running
  commitments demonstrate commitment and time-management even without a
  formal "leadership" story — keep them because they matter to the student,
  not because they need to be strategically framed.

## Guardrail

Four to six meaningful activities, sustained over years, beats twelve. If a
new opportunity comes up, the test is: "does this deepen one of the two main
threads, or is it just another line on a list?" If it's the latter, it's fine
to say no.
