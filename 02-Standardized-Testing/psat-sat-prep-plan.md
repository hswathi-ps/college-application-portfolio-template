# PSAT / SAT Preparation Plan

## The Test Landscape (know this going in)

The SAT is fully digital (the "Bluebook" app), shorter (about 2 hours 14
minutes), and **adaptive** — it adjusts difficulty based on performance in the
first module of each section, and calculators are allowed throughout the math
section. This changes how you prep compared to the old paper SAT: pacing and
early-question accuracy matter more.

**Test-optional policies keep shifting.** Some selective schools have
reinstated testing requirements in recent cycles; others remain test-optional.
Re-check each target school's testing policy in the fall of senior year, not
before — it can change year to year.

## Milestones on the Calendar

| Grade | Test | When | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9th | PSAT 8/9 | Fall (if offered) | Baseline only, no stakes — ask the counselor if it's administered |
| 10th | PSAT 10 | Spring | Practice run, first real data point, no cost |
| 11th | **PSAT/NMSQT** | **October** | **This one counts** — qualifies for National Merit Scholarship. Mark this date as soon as it's announced. |
| 11th | SAT (and/or ACT) | Spring (March–June) | First official attempt — aim to be college-ready in content by now |
| 12th | SAT retake (if needed) | August–October | Superscore or retake to improve; must be done before early application deadlines if applying ED/EA |

## SAT vs. ACT

Take one full-length practice test of each in 10th grade (both are free — via
Bluebook for SAT and the ACT's own practice site) and see which format suits
the student better. Reading-heavy, no-guessing-penalty students often do well
on SAT; ACT has a Science section and a faster pace that suits some students
better. Don't skip the comparison — 45 minutes of diagnostic testing can save
months of prep aimed at the wrong test.

## Free, High-Quality Resources (use these before paying for anything)

1. **Khan Academy Official SAT Practice** — free, built with College Board,
   and it links directly to PSAT/SAT scores to generate a personalized study
   plan. This should be the backbone of prep.
2. **College Board Bluebook app** — the actual test delivery software; also
   hosts official free full-length practice tests. Take these under timed
   conditions to build stamina and pacing.
3. **Official ACT practice tests** (act.org) — if the student leans ACT after
   the diagnostic.
4. The local public library often carries SAT/ACT prep books (Princeton
   Review, Barron's) for free borrowing.

## The Prep Timeline

- **9th–10th grade: no formal test prep.** Instead, build the underlying
  skills that actually move scores — reading widely, strong math fundamentals,
  and light vocabulary-in-context exposure. Forcing SAT prep this early
  usually causes fatigue without much score benefit, since content knowledge
  isn't there yet.
- **Summer before 11th grade:** start structured practice — 3-4 hours/week
  using Khan Academy's personalized plan based on the PSAT 10 results. This is
  also a natural time for a short prep course or tutor if the student wants
  outside structure, but self-study is genuinely sufficient for a strong
  reader with good math fundamentals.
- **Fall of 11th grade:** PSAT/NMSQT in October — treat this as a real dry
  run, not just practice, since National Merit Semifinalist status is a
  genuine admissions and scholarship asset.
- **Winter–Spring of 11th grade:** ramp up to 2-3 timed practice tests,
  targeted review of weak areas, register for the spring SAT.
- **Goal: have a final, good SAT score in hand by June of 11th grade**, so
  senior fall is free for essays and applications instead of test prep.
- **Fall of 12th grade:** one retake window (Aug/Sept/Oct) only if needed to
  hit target range — must land before Early Decision/Early Action deadlines
  (Nov 1) if applying that route.

## Score Targets

Don't set a hard number this early — instead, once real data (PSAT 10,
PSAT/NMSQT) comes in, compare it to the middle-50% test range published on
each target school's Common Data Set to calibrate prep intensity. Test scores
are only one factor among several.

## A Note on Balance

The single biggest lever for a 9th grader isn't SAT prep — it's reading volume
and quality, and strong grades in the core math sequence. Protect time for the
student's actual activities rather than starting SAT bootcamps early; they
compound the real admissions story more than an extra six months of test prep
would.
