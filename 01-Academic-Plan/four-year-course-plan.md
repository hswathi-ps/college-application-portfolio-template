# Four-Year Course Plan

## Why This Matters

Admissions readers evaluate rigor *relative to what your school offers*. The
first thing to do — ideally before scheduling for the next grade — is get your
school's official Program of Studies / course catalog from the counselor, and
identify the full AP/IB/Honors ladder available in each subject. Update this
document with real course names once that catalog is in hand; the shape below
is a general template to adapt.

## 10th Grade — Target Shape

- English: next level up from 9th, aiming toward an AP/IB English course by 11th
- Math: next course in sequence (accelerate if the student is ready)
- Science: pick the AP/Honors science that follows naturally from what they
  enjoyed in 9th
- History/Social Science: whatever's offered at this level (Human Geography,
  Honors Civics/Economics, etc.)
- World Language: continue the sequence — reaching AP-level language by senior
  year is a real asset for both admissions and future study-abroad
- Elective: continue a 4-year thread if there's one worth keeping, or trade up
  to a course tied to the student's actual interest area

## 11th Grade — Target Shape (Most Important Year)

Junior year GPA and course rigor carry the most weight in admissions, since
it's the last full year of grades colleges see at application time.

- English: AP English Language and Composition (or equivalent)
- Math: Precalculus/AP Precalculus, or the start of AP Calculus
- Science: an AP/Honors science based on genuine interest, not just resume padding
- History: the AP History/Government courses that map onto the student's
  interest area, since these give real material for supplemental essays
- World Language: continue toward the AP-level course if on track
- Elective: a good year to add a leadership-heavy elective or a course tied
  directly to the student's "spike" (see `03-Extracurriculars/activity-strategy.md`)

## 12th Grade — Target Shape

- English: AP English Literature and Composition (or equivalent)
- Math: AP Calculus AB/BC or AP Statistics
- Science: a 4th science if scheduling allows (not required, but strengthens the transcript)
- History/Social Science: whatever continues to build the intellectual
  narrative connected to the student's interest area
- World Language: the AP-level course if the sequence supports it
- Electives: whatever deepens the spike — a capstone, independent study, or
  continuation of a specialized pathway
