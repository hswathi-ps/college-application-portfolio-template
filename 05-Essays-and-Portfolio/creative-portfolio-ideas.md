# Creative Portfolio & Essay Material

## Why Build This Now

College essays succeed on specificity — a real detail, a real object, a real
moment — not on abstract claims like "I'm passionate about justice" or "I love
science." The goal over the next four years isn't to manufacture essay
material — it's to **document what the student is already doing** so there's
a real archive to draw from senior year, instead of trying to reconstruct it
from memory.

## Start a Running Archive Now

Create a simple system (a shared photo album, a folder in this repo, or both)
and keep it updated. Adapt this list to whatever the student's actual spike
is:

- **A creative or technical practice** (art, design, music, writing, coding,
  research, building things): document the work as it happens — before/after
  photos, drafts, recordings, code repos, lab notebooks. Note what inspired
  each piece, what techniques were used, what they'd do differently.
- **Reading**: keep a running list of books read, with a line or two on what
  stuck with them from each. This becomes useful for "favorite book"
  supplemental essay prompts, which almost every selective school asks in
  some form.
- **Competitions/performances**: record and date everything — placements,
  recordings, write-ups.

Put this archive in `05-Essays-and-Portfolio/archive/` in this repo (subfolders
by year), or link out to wherever the photos/audio/files actually live if
this repo isn't the right home for large media files.

## Where This Material Can Actually Go in an Application

1. **The Common App Additional Info / Arts supplement**: many schools accept
   an optional arts portfolio or a short creative supplement — check each
   school's supplement requirements senior year; this changes by school and
   year.
2. **The Common App personal essay**: a genuine essay could be built around
   the student's actual practice — what it teaches them about resourcefulness,
   patience, or how it connects (even obliquely) to their academic interest.
3. **"Why us" and activity supplements**: naming a specific program,
   professor, or resource that connects to *both* the academic interest and
   the creative/personal practice shows real research, not a template answer.
4. **Short-answer / "tell us something not on your application" prompts**:
   this is often exactly where a specific, sensory, personal detail belongs.

## Essay Brainstorm Seeds (revisit senior year — don't write anything yet)

- A specific moment that shaped how the student approaches their main
  interest — not the abstract "I've always loved X," but one concrete scene.
- How a long-running discipline (an instrument, a sport, a craft) shaped how
  they approach new skills, including their academic/civic interest.
- A specific project, debate, or piece of work they cared about, and what it
  revealed about how they think.
- The "intersection" essay: is there a real, ownable metaphor connecting the
  student's two main threads? Only worth using if it's true to how they
  actually think, not forced.

## A Note on Authenticity

None of this should be reverse-engineered from "what sounds good to
[dream school]." Keep the archive because it's a genuinely nice record of four
years of real work, and let the essay material emerge from what's actually
true by senior year. The best material here will be whatever the student was
doing anyway, documented well.
